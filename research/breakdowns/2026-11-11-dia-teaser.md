### Frame-level beats

Read from 10-frame strips around 7 cuts, 1 dissolve and 2 moves; 1 f = 41.7 ms at 24 fps.

- **The claim card holds across three picture cuts** (8.25–13.33 s), so the text reads for 5 s while the picture keeps its 0.9 s rhythm.
- **The opening push is barely a move.** ×1.13 over 2.0 s on the Dock: under 1% of scale per frame, linear to the eye. It ends on a hard cut to 'Tomorrow', not on a settle; the cut stops the move.

## 2. Classify

Live-action teaser with the UI rephotographed on a laptop screen. Conventions: macro inserts, rack focus, white cards over picture, a date reveal.

## 3. Diagnose

- Depth of field does the zoom: one control sharp, the rest blurred to colour. → Selective focus as hierarchy: the eye goes to the only sharp thing in frame (Murch's eye-trace).
- Text lives on a layer above the cuts, not inside a shot. → Separating the message track from the picture track lets each keep its own rhythm.
- Shots shorten into the date reveal. → Accelerating rhythm builds anticipation before the payoff.

## 4. Prescribe

- _new_: A card can hold across several picture cuts instead of owning its own shot. (cards (not built))
- _confirms_: End on a logo card; here a teaser's 0.37 s. (CARD.close)

## 5. Critique

- **I like:** The message survives with the sound off: four cards, nine words before the date.
- **I wish:** Not one frame shows what Dia does; by design it teases, so it teaches cards and rhythm, not filming a flow.
- **I wonder:** Could a reel fake depth of field around the point of interest (a blur falloff outside the focused locator) to get the same hierarchy on real footage?

## Not for us

- Live action and a rephotographed screen: cutscene films the app itself, legibly.
