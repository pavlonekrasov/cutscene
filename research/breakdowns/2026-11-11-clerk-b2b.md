## 2. Classify

Motion-designed feature film with real components rebuilt as floating cards. Conventions: claim → component pairs, code-import cards on dark, perspective tilts, a chart finale.

## 3. Diagnose

- Every claim is followed by exactly one component. → One idea per shot: the viewer holds one claim and checks it against one piece of UI.
- Components float with soft shadows on white, no window. → Isolation by figure-ground: the only dark-edged shape on a white field is the subject.
- Perspective tilts appear only in the second half. → Escalation: the film introduces depth once the pattern is learned, to keep a long list from flattening.

## 4. Prescribe

- _confirms_: Isolated components on a plain backdrop, not the whole window. (camera / renderer (not built))
- _new_: Claim → proof pairs as the unit of a feature film. (reel structure)
- _new_: Code cards: an import line typed on dark as the developer's proof. (cards)

## 5. Critique

- **I like:** Nine features in 86 s and none blurs into the next, because the claim → proof pair never varies.
- **I wish:** The rebuilt components are cleaner than any real dashboard; a viewer never sees them in a page.
- **I wonder:** Would a cutscene reel of one flow benefit from claim cards between its acts, kept to three or four?

## Not for us

- 3D perspective tilts of mocked UI: they trade legibility for depth.
