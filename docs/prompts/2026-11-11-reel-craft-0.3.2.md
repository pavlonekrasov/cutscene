# cutscene 0.3.2: reel craft

A brief for the agent that builds it. Pavlo owns cutscene, and the decisions marked as his are not
yours to reopen. Before anything else, read `AGENTS.md`, the Reels section of `GUIDE.md`,
`research/2026-11-08-showreel-references.md` and all of `src/reel/`.

## The goal

cutscene should replace the chain people use to make product videos today: record in Screen Studio
or Recordly, then cut in Premiere Pro or After Effects. A scene file goes in and a reel ready to post
comes out, with no hand-editing in between.

"Ready to post" means a senior motion designer would sign it. Every camera move, speed change and
cut has a reason you can name, and its numbers sit inside the ranges that top-tier reels actually
use. This is for any product cutscene films, not only SC KPI. SC KPI is the first real client and
the hardest test case.

0.3.0 built the machinery: camera, speed ramps, cards and sharp zoom. 0.3.2 gives it judgement:

- a library of the best reels, measured;
- a rulebook drawn from that library;
- a critic that scores every reel against the rulebook;
- the film-editing techniques the evidence supports.

Work as an editor who has spent years cutting launch videos, and who knows why Nolan's editors cut
the way they do. Learn principles from the references. Never copy one reel's look.

## What "AI-made" looks like, named

Pavlo's bar is that the output must not look AI-made. "AI-made" is a vibe. Below are the properties
behind it, each one something the critic can measure or a reviewer can point at on a frame.

| Signature                     | What's actually wrong                                      | The rule                                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The camera never rests        | Every click triggers a move, so the eye never settles      | The camera is still most of the time (measure the share in references); it moves when the action leaves the shot |
| Every move the same           | One zoom and one duration, whatever the distance           | Duration grows with distance; the zoom follows what is framed (working or detail)                                |
| Bounce and overshoot          | An under-damped spring on the camera                       | Critically damped, zero overshoot (0 of 8 measured references overshoot)                                         |
| Blur, trails, whooshes        | Effects standing in for motion quality                     | None on the UI                                                                                                   |
| Tilt and spin on the UI       | A gimmick that resamples text off the pixel grid           | Never during an action (see "Dutch angle" below)                                                                 |
| Inhuman typing and pointer    | 60 chars/s typing; a pointer on one fixed curve and timing | About 17 chars/s on screen; pointer timing follows Fitts's law                                                   |
| A generic backdrop            | Gradient blobs, glass cards                                | The frame's own backdrop colour and the app's own typeface                                                       |
| Every shot centred            | Composition by default, not by intent                      | The point of interest is placed on purpose; across a cut it lands where the eye already is                       |
| Fake UI                       | Mocked screens, made-up products                           | Real app pixels only. cutscene already does this; keep it                                                        |
| Captions explaining the story | The edit doesn't carry it                                  | The cuts and the UI carry the story; cards only open and close the reel                                          |

## Decisions in force

**From 0.3.0, Pavlo's:**

- The reel is a second pass. Scenes record exactly as they do now.
- The API only grows: nothing is renamed or removed, and nothing changes meaning.
- Takes are captured at 2× the zoom, so the camera never upscales.
- Detail shots go to ×2.0 at most.
- Hard cuts between clips. Dissolves only into and out of cards.
- Cards use the app's typeface on the frame's backdrop colour.
- Typing plays at 2×. Push-ins take 500 ms and pull-backs 600 ms.
- A reframe beyond ×1.8, or across more than half the shot, becomes a cut.
- No tilt and no spin. 0.3.2 reopens this one narrowly, as an opt-in only (see the table below).

**New, Pavlo's (2026-11-11):**

## Part 1: Learning from references

This is how cutscene gets better at its craft. Pavlo keeps feeding references in. The agent breaks
each one down frame by frame, and each one has to teach something. The lesson goes into the rulebook,
and the next reel is cut by it.

```
FEED  →  BREAK DOWN  →  LEARN  →  APPLY  →  CHECK
Pavlo     frame by     write the   the rulebook,  Pavlo's
adds a    frame:       lesson      the skill and  verdict on
reel      every cut,   down        the critic     the next reel
          every move
```

An agent remembers nothing between sessions. A lesson counts only once it's written into
`craft.md` and the skill. That's where the learning lives, so a breakdown that ends without a
written lesson hasn't finished.

### 1a. One instrument for references and for our own reels

0.3.0 measured X reels with a throwaway script in the page. Make it a real part of cutscene:

- A pure module, `src/reel/measure.ts`, takes a sequence of small luma frames and returns the
  video's **grammar**, with the same fields for every source:
  - length, shot count, mean and median shot length;
  - each cut: hard, dissolve, or a match (the same element on both sides);
  - each camera move: push, pull or pan, its zoom ratio, its duration, where its speed peaks (as a
    share of the move) and its overshoot;
  - the share of time the camera is still;
  - rhythm: shot length over time, to show whether cutting speeds up toward a climax.
- It has two front ends:
  - in a browser page, over a playing `<video>`, for references (nothing is downloaded);
  - `cutscene measure <file.mp4>`, over frames ffmpeg decodes, for our own reels and any video
    Pavlo has locally. This is a new CLI command, so it's an addition to the API.
- **Validate it first.** On the pan test and the tour reel, where the manifest knows the truth, it
  must recover every move within ±3% zoom; its 10–90% core within ±2 frames; and its 2% and 98%
  edges within ±1% of the travel. Edges are judged by progress, not by frames: on an eased curve
  the first and last 2% take many frames, so a frame count there measures the ease, not the
  instrument. Report the error you measure.

### 1b. The breakdown: frame by frame

The instrument gives the numbers. The breakdown adds what the numbers can't see: what the camera is
doing, and why. For each reference, read the frames around every cut and every move. Seek the
`<video>`, or step through a local file, and keep the stills in scratch. Then write
`research/breakdowns/<date>-<slug>.md` with these parts.

**Shot list.** One row per shot, with:

- its timecode and length;
- its framing: wide, working or detail, with the zoom;
- what's being acted on;
- how it starts and ends: a hard cut, a dissolve or a match cut;
- any move inside it, with numbers (×1.4 push, 500 ms, ease-in-out, speed peak at 0.45);
- whether typing or waiting is sped up;
- where the pointer and the point of interest sit at each cut.

**Frame-level beats.** The timing that makes a moment work, counted in frames. For example: "the
camera starts 9 frames before the pointer sets off and settles 4 frames before the press"; "the
cut lands on the frame the panel finishes opening".

**Why it works.** In precise terms, with no vibe words: name the property a developer could change,
not "smooth" or "clean".

**What it teaches.** Each lesson gets one of these tags:

- _confirms_ a rule (its n goes up by one);
- _adjusts_ a rule (the old range, then the new one);
- _new_: a technique or a beat the rulebook doesn't have yet, which stays provisional until 3
  references show it;
- _contradicts_ a rule, which goes to Pavlo.

**Not for us.** What the reel does that cutscene shouldn't copy, and why: its brand look, a
live-action insert, a trick that breaks legibility.

Also record by eye, per reference:

- zoom levels (how much of the window shows);
- cards: count, length, style;
- the cursor: size and look;
- the frame: a window on a backdrop, or full-bleed;
- the aspect ratio;
- sound: present or not, and whether the cuts land on the beat.

### 1c. Sources

- **Pavlo's inbox, first.** `research/inbox.md` is where he drops what he finds, one per line: an X
  post, a YouTube or Vimeo link, a page, or a path to a local file. He can add a note, for example
  "look at how they handle the modal". The note is the question the breakdown has to answer. Once a
  reference is broken down, its line moves to a "Done" list with a link to its breakdown. X links
  wait for a session he signs in to.

- **X**, as in 0.3.0:
  - a headed, persistent profile kept in scratch, which Pavlo signs in to himself;
  - read only: no likes, follows, replies, posts, DMs, settings changes or downloads;
  - never type a credential, and never accept or bypass a CAPTCHA;
  - delete the profile afterwards.
- **Sources with no sign-in**, so most of the routine runs without Pavlo:
  - launch pages and changelogs that host their video (Linear, Vercel, Raycast, Figma, Notion,
    Stripe, Arc/Dia, Framer);
  - those companies' YouTube and Vimeo channels;
  - Screen Studio's own showcase. It's the tool we're replacing, so learn its defaults;
  - Mobbin (Pavlo's saves; they were stills only in 0.3.0).
- **The craft canon, as principles to cite, not videos to copy:**
  - Walter Murch, _In the Blink of an Eye_: the Rule of Six (emotion, story, rhythm, eye-trace,
    the 2D plane, 3D space) and cutting on action;
  - Disney's slow in / slow out and anticipation;
  - Fitts's law and minimum-jerk motion (Flash & Hogan, 1985) for the pointer;
  - Nolan's films for parallel action and format changes, as Part 2 describes.
- **What "top-tier" means:** a judgement about craft, with a reason you can name. For example,
  "Figma's camera is settled before the edit starts". Likes filter candidates; they never justify
  one. Keep rejected candidates with the reason (live action, an interview).
- Read in the browser only. No video files saved. Stills stay in scratch.

### 1d. Where it lives

- `research/inbox.md`: what Pavlo feeds in, and what's been done.
- `research/references/<date>-<slug>.json`: one file per reel, holding the measured grammar, the
  source URL, the date read and why it's top-tier. Stills are never committed.
- `research/breakdowns/<date>-<slug>.md`: the frame-by-frame breakdown and its lessons.
- `research/craft.md`: the rulebook. Each rule gives the rule and its range, **n** (the number of
  references that support it, and which ones), and the cutscene setting it maps to. A rule with
  n < 3 is marked provisional.
- `research/verdicts.md`: Pavlo's taste log. Each time he accepts or rejects a reel, a shot or a
  rule, add one line: what it was, his words, and the rule it confirms or changes.
- `skills/cutscene/craft.md`: the rulebook, distilled for the skill, so agents in other projects
  direct from it. `research/` doesn't ship; `skills/` does.

Say it plainly in the docs: the AI's learning lives in these files. The breakdowns hold what each
reel taught. `craft.md` and the skill carry it into every reel. Pavlo's verdicts correct it.

### 1e. The routine

- **This task:**
  - Break down the 8 reels from 0.3.0 again, frame by frame. They have numbers but no breakdowns.
  - Grow the set to at least 20 reels, with at least 8 from sources that need no sign-in.
  - If any top-tier reel uses parallel action, a format change or sound, find at least 3 of them.
- **On demand:** when Pavlo says "break down the inbox", or drops in a reference with a note,
  process it in that session.
- **Monthly:** a run on a local scheduled task, not a cloud one, because X needs Pavlo's sign-in on
  this machine.
  - It processes the inbox and the sources that need no sign-in, and asks Pavlo before it opens X.
  - It breaks down each new reference, then updates `craft.md` and the skill.
- **Every run ends with "What we learned":**
  - the new lessons, each with its tag;
  - the rules whose ranges moved;
  - any technique that reached n = 3, proposed as a default;
  - any contradiction.
- A change that would move a default goes to Pavlo. It never goes straight into code.
- Propose the schedule. Create it only after Pavlo says yes.

## Part 2: The film grammar, translated

**How verdicts work:**

- **Default:** needs evidence from at least 3 top-tier references, and a passing critic.
- **Opt-in:** needs 1–2 references or Pavlo's explicit ask, and the technique must keep the app
  sharp and legible.
- **Rejected:** anything that breaks sharpness or legibility.

| Technique                                                                                                        | What it does in a product reel                                                                                                                                                  | The realistic version                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Verdict                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cross-cutting, parallel action** (Dunkirk's three timelines converging; Oppenheimer's two intercut timelines)  | Shows two people's sessions when one person's action changes the other's screen (Media suggests, Admin approves)                                                                | **`cross`:** alternate shots from two takes, paired by marks from cause to effect. Shots get shorter toward the moment the two sessions meet, then one long hold on the result. **`split`:** two panes side by side, synced on a shared mark. Only one pane acts at a time while the other holds, and each pane frames its working region, not the whole window. At most 2 sessions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Build in 0.3.2: Pavlo asked for it, and the SC KPI reel needs it                                                                            |
| **Non-linear timeline** (Memento's reverse chronology)                                                           | Reversed, a demo can't teach cause and effect, so the viewer never learns the flow                                                                                              | **Cold open:** open on the result for 1–1.5 s, then cut to the start: "here's where we end up, here's how"                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Reverse chronology rejected. Cold open opt-in in 0.3.2; a default only with evidence                                                        |
| **Dutch angle, the rotating corridor** (Inception)                                                               | In film it signals unease, the opposite of a product reel's job. Rotation resamples text off the pixel grid (soft, shimmering edges), and the horizon is the reader's reference | Never on anything being acted on, modal dialogs included. At most, a roll of 3° or less on an establishing wide shot or a card, settled to 0° before the first action                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | Rejected for UI in action. The settle is opt-in only if references show it; the gate checks every action frame is at 0°                     |
| **IMAX aspect switch** (Nolan cuts between IMAX 1.43:1 / 1.90:1 and 2.20:1 / 2.39:1 to open the frame for scale) | A format change that marks a beat: the frame opens when the product appears                                                                                                     | **Matte:** open letterboxed at 2.39:1 on the card and the establishing shot, then open to 16:9 at the first action over 600–800 ms (ease-in-out), with bars in the backdrop colour, not black. **Delivery formats:** the same edit at 16:9, 1:1 and 4:5, with the camera re-planned per format from the same action data. A 4:5 frame can't show a desktop window legibly, so it uses working shots only.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Matte opt-in in 0.3.2. Delivery formats: research first, then build only on Pavlo's go (each format adds 10–15 min of render per 40 s reel) |
| **Shepard tone** (the rising-pitch illusion in Dunkirk's score)                                                  | Tension rising into a reveal                                                                                                                                                    | Silent stays the default, because most feeds autoplay muted. Sound is unmeasured so far: in 0.3.0 only shadcn's reel was checked (it has a track), and the other seven were read muted. **Supplied track:** `audio: "<file>"`, a track Pavlo has the rights to, with cuts snapped to the nearest beat within ±2 frames. **Riser:** an optional Shepard-tone riser, generated deterministically (an OfflineAudioContext in Chromium, or Node), ending exactly on the reveal cut. Never imitate any composer's music. Adding sound must not change the video stream or the gate's numbers.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           | Supplied track: build if 3 or more references cut on the beat. Riser opt-in, last in priority                                               |
| **Practical effects, no CGI** ("drop a real mouse from 1.2 m to measure gravity")                                | The real principle: motion comes from measuring the real world, not from a curve someone typed in                                                                               | **Already practical:** every pixel is the real app, recorded, never mocked. Say so in the rulebook. **The pointer:** today it glides on a cubic ease-in-out over 260 ms + 0.45 ms/px (clamped to 320–1000 ms) with a slight arc. Real hands follow Fitts's law, MT = a + b·log₂(D/W + 1), with a minimum-jerk speed profile (10k³ − 15k⁴ + 6k⁵) and a small correction at the end of a long move to a small target. Calibrate on a real hand: Pavlo clicks about 40 targets in a test page, about 5 minutes, and you fit a and b from his trace; without it, use published constants and cite them. **The camera:** the follow left unbuilt in 0.3.0. A critically damped spring tracks the action point outside the dead zone, tuned to settle inside the measured push range (400–570 ms), with overshoot measured at exactly 0. | Spring follow: build in 0.3.2. Human pointer: build as an opt-in for reel takes only, so scenes keep today's pointer                        |
| **Match cut, eye-trace** (Murch's eye-trace; Nolan's match cuts)                                                 | A hard cut the eye doesn't feel                                                                                                                                                 | **Eye-trace:** on every hard cut, place the incoming shot so its point of interest lands within about 10% of the frame's width of where the outgoing one was. The camera knows both points. **Match cut:** the same element in two contexts (a badge in the list, then the same badge on the record page) at the same screen position and size, set with a mark in each clip                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Eye-trace: build as a default in 0.3.2. Match cut: opt-in                                                                                   |

## Part 3: What 0.3.2 builds, in order

At each step: typecheck, build, re-record the example (desktop and phone, light and dark), re-render
the reels, compare the critic's numbers before and after, read the stills, then commit and push at
the green checkpoint.

1. **The instrument and the learning loop** (Part 1): the measure module and its validation; the
   inbox; 20+ references, each broken down frame by frame; `craft.md`, `verdicts.md` and the
   skill's copy; and the first "What we learned".
   **Stop:** report how the rulebook's ranges compare with today's defaults, and propose verdicts
   for Part 2. Pavlo confirms or changes them before you build anything else.
2. **The critic.** Each reel's `manifest.json` gains a `craft` section: every measured property,
   its range from `craft.md`, and whether it's inside the range. Out of range is a warning; Pavlo
   decides later which warnings become failures. The motion-fps warning moves here.
   - Run it on the 0.3.1 tour reel first. That's the baseline.
3. **The spring follow and eye-trace cuts**, as reel defaults. `camera.follow: "moves"` keeps the
   0.3.0 behaviour.
4. **The human pointer**, opt-in for reel takes.
5. **Parallel action:** `cross`, `split` and the cold open.
6. **The opt-ins:** the matte, the supplied track with beat snapping, the riser and the Dutch
   settle. Each one goes only as far as its verdict allows.
7. **Delivery formats**, only on Pavlo's go.

## Proof

- **Scenes don't move.** On the example app (desktop and phone, light and dark), PSNR, colour shift
  and off share are identical to 0.3.1: 52.7 / 46.9 dB light, 48.9 / 45.6 dB dark. Durations stay
  within 0.3.1's own spread between runs.
- **Reels get better, and you can show it:**
  - render the tour reel and the pan test on 0.3.1 and on 0.3.2, and give a table of the critic's
    scores for both;
  - the gate passes at 38 dB or more at the whole frame and at the deepest zoom;
  - `cutscene measure` finds zero overshoot in the spring follow;
  - the pan test still steps on no frame.
- **Every take reaches 60 fps of motion, or it's recorded again.** The 0.3.1 re-run hit 51.6 fps on
  a loaded machine, and the example reel's pace has no headroom: it measured exactly 60 on a quiet
  run. Record on a quiet machine, and give the example reel's pace about 10% of margin.
- **New techniques are proven on frames.** For each one, give a frame strip around where it acts,
  plus the contact sheet and the mark stills.
  - The example app has one user and saves nothing, so it proves the mechanics of `cross` and
    `split` (two takes, synced marks).
  - The real test is SC KPI flow 3: Media suggests, the Admin approves. That waits for Pavlo's go to
    copy cutscene into SC KPI.
- **The package still installs.** `bin/` changes for `cutscene measure`, so run the installed-package
  test: `npm pack`, a fresh project, `init`, a first scene, a first reel and `measure`.

## House rules

- Learn principles, never one reel's look. No downloads and no reproduction. Stills stay in scratch.
  Quote numbers, not frames.
- Honest framing and fictional data.
- Renders take about 0.45 s per 4K frame, so a 40 s reel takes 10–15 minutes. Run long jobs in the
  background.
- Stage files by path. Push as commits land. Never force-push.
